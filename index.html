Build and test master rosters against the blueprint and the rules you set: a minimum rest between shifts, a cap on shifts in a row, contracted hours only, and weekends spread out. Start from a blank roster, set your own shift times, edit any shift, or let the optimiser suggest changes.

Quick reference

How to use

  • 1. Choose a starting roster. The Starting roster dropdown: a blank roster, or one you saved earlier. Add your own lines and set your shift times.
  • 2. Set your roster settings, shift times and blueprint. Roster settings holds cover hours, shifts per week, minimum rest, max shifts in a row and the unpaid break. Then pick a template or edit each shift’s start/finish time and how many lines are needed per day. This changes every rostered shift of that type at once.
  • 3. Edit or optimise. Change any cell directly, add or remove lines, tick Lock to protect a line, or click Optimise for suggested changes (review before keeping).
  • 4. Review, save, export. Check the scorecard, Save the roster under a name, and export a CSV for Excel or a file to keep or share.

What Optimise checks

  • Fills blueprint gaps first, weighted so high-priority shifts are covered before low-priority ones.
  • Caps runs at your maximum shifts in a row, and limits how many weekends in a row part-time and full-time lines can work.
  • Requires at least your minimum rest (hours) between the end of one shift and the start of the next.
  • Limits part-time Sunday work, and checks that every half-hour of your set “cover hours” has someone on.
  • Favours grouping days off together: a lone rest day boxed in by working days on both sides (across the whole rotation, wrapping week to week) counts against a roster, so runs of 2+ days off are preferred where the blueprint allows it.
  • Weighs each shift type’s gaps by its Weight × setting on top of its priority, so you can push the optimiser to close one shift’s shortfalls before another’s.
  • Favours an even spread of paid hours across lines of the same type this printed week — a line running noticeably heavier or lighter than the others counts against the roster.
  • Tries direct swaps between two lines (one gives up a shift it doesn’t need that day, another picks it up on a day it does) as well as moving a single line’s own shifts — swaps usually clear a vacancy faster than a chain of one-line moves.
  • Makes the smallest set of changes that still improves the roster — it won’t reshuffle lines that don’t need to move. Tick “Minimise changes when optimising” to weigh this much more heavily, for a smaller, gentler suggestion.
  • Keeps shifts matching inside a run of working days, with the latest shift last: 07:30, 07:30, 08:30, 12:00 is ideal. Starting earlier the day after a later shift counts against a roster, and so does hopping between different shifts.
  • Keeps the line structure you chose in Settings (rotation average, every line, or off).
  • Never moves Locked, Casual or Overtime lines.
  • Fills gaps from the vacant lines where it can: when real staff can pick up a shift that was sitting on a vacant line, it moves it across. Whatever it can’t cover with your rostered lines is parked on a vacant line (existing ones first, a new one only if needed), so it shows on the overtime offer list ready to offer out. Vacant shifts never count as cover.

Tips

  • The roster grid hard-blocks your minimum rest rule: after you assign a shift, the next day’s dropdown for that line only shows shifts that leave enough of a break (e.g. with 10.5 hours of rest, after a 12:00–22:00 shift only 08:30 or later is offered the next day). Hover a restricted cell to see why options are missing.
  • Empty cells are also steered toward open core coverage gaps (amber highlight): if the scorecard’s Core cover check is failing for a day, blank cells that day only offer the shift(s) still short — fill that gap (here or on another line) to unlock the rest of the shift menu again. Soft/low-priority shortfalls don’t restrict, since those are expected to sometimes go unfilled. Untick “Steer empty cells toward coverage gaps” above the roster to turn this off and get the full shift menu everywhere.
  • When a scorecard check shows Review or Fails, its detail names the specific lines and days behind it (for example which line breaks the rest rule, or which day is a lone day off), so you know where to look in the grid.
  • Set the “Cover hours” (in the Roster settings panel) to the hours you need covered — this drives the cover check in the scorecard.
  • The Shift coverage table marks the single costliest unfilled gap with a star (★) — the one shortfall doing the most damage to the score, worth fixing first if you’re editing by hand.
  • To fill a vacant shift with a casual or overtime pickup: add a Casual or Overtime line, check the overtime offer list for what’s still open, then assign that shift to the new line directly in the grid and type the person’s name into its Staff name column.
  • For a fully custom roster: start from “Blank roster”, set your shift times and blueprint, then add lines.
  • Nothing is saved automatically except what you Save or Set as default (stored in this browser only) — use Export roster file for a backup you can keep or move to another computer.
